You Can Do It

Experimental Design · 2026

You Can Do It is a reward-less clicker about working under economic pressure. You repeat an empty task to hit a daily quota, and every day you hit it, the quota goes up based on your own output. There's no progression and no victory, just the question of how many days you can keep up before the quota becomes impossible.

The game doesn't explain any of this through story or dialogue. It's built around a kind of reversed rubber-banding, where the game watches how much you give and keeps raising the bar so you never get ahead, like an economy that turns any improvement against you. I kept the main action down to a single click on purpose, to echo how repetitive, mechanical work can end up disconnected from whatever the goal was supposed to be.

One challenge was turning physical exhaustion into something the player actually feels, not just a stamina bar they watch. Your energy controls the speed of your own cursor: moving it drains energy, and a drained cursor gets slower and harder to control. Standing still recharges it, but costs you time you don't have. The tiredness shows up in your hands, in how clumsy the cursor gets, not in a number on the screen.
