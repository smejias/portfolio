My contributions

I worked on Frozen Adventures across four roles over three years (QA Lead → QA Manager → Product Manager → Game Designer), giving me an end-to-end perspective on the title.

As Game Designer

  • Created a content pipeline that reused existing assets to produce new stories faster and at lower cost
  • Analyzed and rebalanced soft currency sinks to improve long-term economy health
  • Collaborated with art and writing teams to execute content from concept through release
  • Designed narrative content for 3 releases

As Product Manager

  • Managed backlog and roadmap for a team of 18+
  • Designed and implemented a new 3D content production pipeline
  • Presented KPIs and seasonal content plans to Disney stakeholders
  • Designed and analyzed AB Tests to improve retention and monetization
  • Shipped content across 12+ live updates over a year
  • Designed seasonal Live Ops events (Christmas events and sales) and general content (new stories and rooms to decorate), from franchise research through Disney pitch to release

The context

My journey at Jam City started in QA on games like Harry Potter: Hogwarts Mystery and Snoopy Pop. I quickly moved into leadership, helping build and lead the QA team for Disney Emoji Blitz, the first Disney game our studio took on. That went well, so I was asked to also lead QA for Frozen Adventures while it was still in development. As more Disney games came our way, I was promoted to QA Manager, overseeing all Disney QA teams in Buenos Aires. This solidified my experience working with major IP holders and scaling teams.

When our Buenos Aires studio fully took over Frozen Adventures, I saw an opportunity to expand my skill set and moved into a Product Manager role. I led a team of 18+ people, navigating post-pandemic challenges like reduced headcount and technical constraints. During this time, I designed a new pipeline for creating high-quality 3D content faster and more affordably. I also found myself increasingly doing narrative design work, pitching storylines directly to Disney.

Eventually I transitioned into Game Designer, a natural evolution given my deep knowledge of the game from both QA and product perspectives. Frozen Adventures became the project where I grew from tester to designer, and each role shaped how I approach game development today.
