My contributions

As Quest Designer, I owned the complete quest development pipeline from concept to release.

The context

I joined Bisonic to focus specifically on quest design, something I'd been doing informally in previous roles but wanted to own fully. This was my first time working on an MMORPG and my first experience with Web3 game development. What stood out was the team's commitment to making Forgotten Runiverse a real game first, with Web3 elements supporting rather than defining the experience.

The role gave me full ownership of a content vertical. I pitched quest concepts, documented designs, built them in-engine, and iterated based on feedback. Working with a smaller team meant wearing multiple hats, so I also contributed to narrative design process improvements and FTUE analysis beyond my core quest work.

The quests I designed were live and playable until the game was retired in 2026. Several community members recorded walkthrough videos of the content, linked below.

A branching quest: Mining Rights

Mining Rights is one of the first quests a player meets. It introduces the Red Mages, one of the game's seven schools of magic: ambitious, business-minded and a little cyberpunk. Their emissary, Alfus, sends the player to take back a mine from one of his own attendants, a corporate takeover in miniature. I used it to let players choose how to solve the problem, and to tempt them into acting like a Red Mage.

The flow below is drawn for this page. It follows the quest's real branches, with the dialogue summarized in my own words and a few short lines quoted from the in-game script.

  1. Step 1

    The contract

    The pitchAlfus sells the job as an opportunity. His attendant Gaines has seized an “abandoned” mine to the southwest, which the Red Capital's own law makes illegal. Alfus wants him removed, and wants a word with him.
    The small printThe player signs a contract, a key item described as having “a lot fine print”, and gets the Heat Wave recipe up front. Alfus hands it over with a line about what a little fire can accomplish.
  2. Step 2

    Two ways in

    The front gateA bandit guards the entrance and tells the player to leave. The player can fight through.
    The secret entranceA talking animal near the entrance recognizes players who lifted the curse on the wood in an earlier quest. Some jerks moved into the mine, it says, and it offers to show them its private entrance. They skip straight to Step 4.
  3. Step 3 · front gate only

    Inside the mine

    The campThick tents that look like they could burn, and a group of bandits blocking the next door. The captain gives the player one chance to leave alive.
    Heat WaveThe player can set the tents on fire. Two guards come running to deal with the flames, which divides the group. The battles are different depending on whether the tents were burned.
  4. Step 4 · first choice

    Gaines

    He points out that the man who hired the player did not offer the best deal, and proposes his own: he walks away, and the player keeps what he found.

    No dealThe player fights his bodyguards. Gaines gives up and says he will report to Alfus himself.
    Take the dealGaines hands over a stash of materials and hopes their paths cross again someday, to do some proper business.
  5. Step 5 · second choice

    Back with Alfus

    Alfus asks how events played out, and the player picks what to tell him.

    “The mine is yours”Gaines came back begging forgiveness, and Alfus sent him to the Capital Review Board. Alfus pays 150 gold plus a bonus recipe, the Hip Shades.
    “He was already gone”A lie. Alfus wonders aloud whether more investigation is needed, then pays the full 150 gold.
    “I let him go”The truth, after taking the deal. Alfus is shocked, then notes the contract had no clause demanding Gaines's return. He pays 50 gold and says he will read the fine print next time.
    the small print decides the argument
  6. Epilogue

    The camp remembers

    If Gaines was turned inAn attendant back at the Red camp hopes the Review Board gives him what he deserves.
    If he was let goAn attendant at the Red camp can't believe Gaines evaded justice, then starts to doubt their own read of the risk and reward.
    Alfus, at the endHe tells the player the Red Hats could use an enterprising wizard, and points them to another job close by.
  7. Planned

    A loose thread

    A Gaines who got away was meant to turn up again in the Red Mages' city, which was planned for a later update.
  • Choices that fit the schoolTaking a better deal, bending the truth and reading the fine print are what a Red Mage would do, so the branches express the faction instead of just describing it. Every branch pays and none ends the quest badly.
  • The small print pays offThe contract looks like a joke item, but it decides the argument in the “I let him go” branch. It is also an item I could reuse in later quests.
  • The tool comes with the questAlfus hands over the Heat Wave recipe up front, and Heat Wave is exactly what the tent shortcut asks for. Players who connect the two get an easier fight.
  • Reuse at small scaleThe layout follows the Team Rocket hideouts of the first Pokémon games, shrunk to two chambers and a final boss. The secret entrance links it to an earlier quest.
  • Staging around engine limitsNPCs could not walk, so they had to be teleported from one spot to another. I staged scenes with fades and transition animations so every cut made sense.
